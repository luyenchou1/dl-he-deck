Use Case · Academic Integrity · High School & Career Programs

Verifying authentic understanding at the point of submission.

For a large accredited online school serving high-school completion, career-diploma, and associate programs, DeweyLearn confirms work is the student's own, and that they understand it.

A student submits a written assignment. DeweyLearn scores it against the rubric, then DeweyBot (our conversational agent) runs a short, structured AI interview.

It probes three things: authenticity (is this their own work), narrative congruency (do spoken explanations match the writing), and depth of understanding (can they explain the concepts).

The rubric score and full transcript go back to faculty, who retain sole responsibility for grading and integrity decisions.

DeweyBot interview · excerpt

DeweyBot

Privacy & governance by design. Most learners are adults; some are minors. The interview is short, structured, and faculty-supervised. Transcripts and scores go only to faculty, who make every call. No student work, transcript, or interaction record persists after delivery.

Use Case · Veterinary Technology · Accreditation

Programs must verify externship and clinical-placement sites meet accreditation standards. Site visits are slow and capacity-limited, so coverage stays thin and inconsistent.

DeweyLearn assesses sites from video against the accreditation rubric the program is held to. More sites get reviewed in less time, and with higher, more consistent fidelity than visit-by-visit review can sustain.

Every site is scored against the same criteria, so programs can show accreditors coverage and consistency with evidence attached, not anecdote.

Use Case · Nursing · Clinical Simulation

Standardizing standardized simulations.

Validated instruments like the Creighton Competency Evaluation Instrument (C-CEI) standardize how nursing simulations are scored, but running and scoring them by hand is heavy on instructor time and load.

DeweyLearn observes the simulation and scores it against the instrument the program already uses. That cuts the time and cognitive load of running and grading sims, so instructors run more, score faster, and stay consistent.

Students get iterative, referenced feedback tied to specific moments and aligned to a clinical-readiness framework, not generic commentary.

The instrument standardizes judgment. We make that standardization achievable at scale.
